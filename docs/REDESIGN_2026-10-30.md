# Connected context — local redesign

## Reference inspection

Primary reference: `design-references/interaction-reference.mp4`, 1918 × 1078,
30 fps, 56.93 seconds. OpenCV decoded it successfully. A twenty-frame overview
and three twelve-frame sequential sheets are saved locally in
`design-references/analysis/` (ignored by Git).

Observed moments:

- 0–2 s: a small centered Korean headline, very dark particulate field, large
  surrounding negative space, compact fixed navigation.
- 2.5–4 s: the laptop image enters below the headline, takes over the central
  frame, and continues upward with the scroll. At 4.5–5.5 s the next white
  surface displaces the dark scene vertically.
- 6.0–7.5 s: thin outlines and cobalt accents accumulate on a three-row list.
- 9.5–10.5 s: white panels move upward and out of a stack; the cobalt image
  panel replaces them in the same focal region. Foreground/background overlap
  continues during the change; the section does not switch in one hard cut.
- 18.5–19 s: the two large panels change their faces as the pointer moves.
  This is visible pointer feedback; a destination click is not demonstrated.
- 20.5–22 s: character images replace one another within the same paper frame.
  The recording does not establish the exact trigger, so no hidden click or
  drag behavior is claimed.
- 29–32 s: a large character, short heading and compact action resolve the
  page. The action has visibly different filled/outlined hover states.
- 35–54 s: reverse scrolling revisits the same sections and visual identities.

The reference's numerical easing curves and animation durations cannot be
recovered reliably from frames plus unknown scroll input. The implementation
uses decelerating entrances, overlapping movement and scroll-linked continuity
as an interpretation of the observed qualities, not as a measured reproduction.

## Creative translation

The new identity uses near-black, off-white and cobalt, large Korean headlines,
an original chrome/cobalt linked sculpture and deliberate edge cropping. The
sculpture represents the connection between a cited source and a document.
The reference's astronaut, brand and proprietary images are not reused.

The opening and change demonstration were rendered first in Chromium. The first
hero screenshot exposed a collision between white lettering and a chrome
highlight. Lowering the sculpture separated those focal points. The document
responses were checked in the browser before extending the page.

The full page varies its structure: an oversized opening, a hands-on change
scene, a contextual review walkthrough, a cobalt metadata disclosure, centered
installation, ruled limitations and a typographic conclusion. No scene pins the
page or intercepts scrolling. Mobile uses its own vertical demo, compact image
crop and disclosure navigation.

### Final generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: original sculptural image for an open source source-citation and document-review website, not a UI mockup.
Create a dramatic high-end CGI studio still of two massive physically interlocked elongated rounded chain links, one mirror-polished liquid chrome and one saturated electric cobalt blue ceramic. They represent a source file and its cited document linked by evidence. Sculptural and exceptionally simple: exactly two thick continuous rounded rectangular links with large open holes. The chrome link extends diagonally from upper left toward center, the cobalt link extends from center to lower right; interlocking at center with physically convincing overlap, strong depth. Asymmetric dynamic diagonal composition, objects occupy 85% of frame, centered with all outer contours inside image. 3/4 perspective, reflective chrome with long softbox highlights and dark charcoal reflections, cobalt smooth glossy lacquer. Pitch black seamless background #09090b, no floor horizon, faint subtle blue light on lower side. Art-directed museum object photography, crisp edges, controlled highlights, tactile machined material. Landscape 3:2. No text, no logos, no UI, no stars, no particles, no cards, no people, no watermark.
